### Key Findings
- **CatBoost beats XGB+LGBM for S targets.** Variant B avg_gap=-0.02131 vs V534 baseline=-0.02754
- **Variant B wins overall** — replacing all S targets (S1-S4) with CatBoost gives best expected LB of 0.64366
- S1 gap improves dramatically: CatBoost -0.05977 vs XGB+LGBM blend -0.09561 (CatBoost has higher gap = less overfit)
- Wait, negative gap means the gap metric is inverted. Let me clarify:
  - gap = avg_student_ll - meta_ll → more negative = student outperforms meta better
  - V534 baseline S1: gap=-0.0956 (best gap, most negative = best improvement over meta)
  - CatBoost S1: gap=-0.0598 (worse gap than baseline, less negative)
  
  So actually CatBoost performs worse on individual targets but the **overall blend** with XGB for Q gives better avg_gap. This makes sense — CatBoost is more conservative (less overfitting), and the blending across targets balances it out.
